Take it or skip it

Lesson 1 · Dynamic programming · about 15 minutes. One win: write your first DP recurrence and fill its table.

Bite 1 · Plain recursion repeats itself

The Fibonacci rule is F(n) = F(n−1) + F(n−2). Computed straight from that rule, F(5) asks for F(3) twice, F(2) three times, and F(1) five times. The same small questions get answered again and again, and the time blows up to exponential.

The fix is to write each answer down the first time you compute it, in an array. After that, looking it up is free. Fibonacci drops to O(n).

Source: your notes, lec13_10_08_26.typ ("Why so slow?"), and Erickson, Chapter 3.

Bite 2 · DP has two parts

1A recurrence. This is the hard, creative part, and it is what makes the answer correct.

2A table, filled in the right order. This is the mechanical part, and it is what makes the answer fast.

The trick for part 1: make one decision. Try every option. Each option leaves a smaller copy of the same problem, and you trust that copy to be solved (the recursion fairy).

Bite 3 · One worked example

Problem. You get an array A[1..n]. Pick numbers, but never two that sit next to each other. Make the sum as big as possible.

Name what you compute. MaxSum(i) = the biggest sum you can pick from A[i..n].

The one decision: stand at position i. Do you take A[i]?

OptionWhat you getWhere you continue
Skip A[i]nothingi+1 (next spot)
Take A[i]A[i]i+2 (i+1 is banned)

Write both options as one line, then add the base case:

MaxSum(i) = max( MaxSum(i+1),  A[i] + MaxSum(i+2) )
MaxSum(i) = 0    if i > n          ⟨⟨nothing left to pick⟩⟩
Answer: MaxSum(1)

Fill the table. Each entry needs the entries to its right, so fill from right to left. Here A = [5, 1, 1, 5]:

The answer is 10 (take both 5s). There are n entries and each one costs O(1), so the running time is O(n).

The question that writes every recurrence: "After this choice, where am I allowed to continue?" That position goes inside the recursive call.

Practice · Your turn

1. You skip A[i]. Which call gives the best from there on?

Skipping bans nothing, so you continue at the very next spot.

2. You take A[i]. You get A[i] plus which call?

Taking A[i] bans i+1, so the first free spot is i+2.

3. New rule: taking A[i] bans both i+1 and i+2. You take A[i]. Plus which call?

Two spots are banned, so the first free one is i+3. Only this one call changes; the rest of the recurrence stays the same.

5. Which call answers the whole problem?

MaxSum(1) means "best from A[1..n]", which is the whole array.

6. Fill the table for A = [2, 7, 9, 3, 1], right to left. MaxSum(6) and MaxSum(7) are 0.

5: max(0, 1+0) = 1 · 4: max(1, 3+0) = 3 · 3: max(3, 9+1) = 10 · 2: max(10, 7+3) = 10 · 1: max(10, 2+10) = 12. The best pick is 2 + 9 + 1.

7. Why do you fill this table right to left?

MaxSum(i) reads MaxSum(i+1) and MaxSum(i+2), so those must be filled first.

8. Running time: n entries, each a max of two numbers. Total?

(number of entries) × (time per entry) = n × O(1).
