* Lecture 01 - Introduction to CS 374

*Important Terms*

** Alphabets

Let \Sigma be an *alphabet*, an arbitrary *finite* set of *symbols* or *characters*

Examples include:
- \Sigma $= \{ A, B, \dots, Z \}$
- \Sigma $= \{0, 1\}$

** Strings

A *string* or *word* is a *finite* sequence of symbols from an alphabet

Formally speaking, $w$ is a string over \Sigma if it is one of
- the *empty string*
- an ordered pair $\left(a, x\right)$, where $a \in \Sigma$ and $x$ is a string over \Sigma

*** Example

\begin{align*}
 STRING &= (S, (TRING)) \\
 &= (S, (T, (RING))) \\
 &= \dots \\
 &= (S, (T, (R, (I, (N, (G, \varepsilon))))))
\end{align*}

** Functions

*Functions* are defined recursively, where length $|w|$ represents
the number of symbols in $w$

\[
\lvert w \rvert := \begin{cases}
  0 & \text{ if } w = \varepsilon \\
  1 + \lvert x \rvert & \text{ if } w = a \cdot x
\end{cases}
\]

*** Example

\begin{align*}
\lvert HAT \rvert &= 1 + \lvert AT \rvert \\
&= 1 + (1 + \lvert T \rvert) \\
&= 1 + (1 + (1 + \lvert \varepsilon \rvert)) \\
&= \boxed{3}
\end{align*}

** Concatenation

 Let *concatenation*, $w \cdot z$, of strings $w$ and $z$ be represented as
 \[
    w \cdot z := \begin{cases}
    z & \text{ if } w = \varepsilon \\
    (a, x \cdot z) & \text{ if } w = a \cdot z
    \end{cases}
 \]

*** Example

\begin{align*}
NOW \cdot HERE &= N \cdot (OW \cdot HERE) \\
&= N \cdot (O \cdot (W \cdot HERE)) \\
&= N \cdot (O \cdot (W \cdot (\varepsilon \cdot HERE))) \\
&= N \cdot (O \cdot (W \cdot HERE)) \\
&= NOWHERE \\
\end{align*}

- *Explicitly* state a *strong* inductive hypothesis (i.e. ~for all k < n~
